The 2 km Loop: fixing Nallurahalli's choke points

Residents, Whitefield Rising and the Brookefields-Kundalahalli Residents Welfare Trust are clearing the Shiva Temple – Munneswara Temple – Brigade IRV – Ambedkar Statue loop near Nallurhalli Metro.

● Phase 2 · Active

Free the Shiva Temple Junction

Electric poles stand inside the carriageway at the junction of New Temple Road and Nallurahalli Main Road. Traffic has to swerve around them, and people on foot walk in the road. We will take the cables underground and remove 6 poles.

What this phase does, and what it doesn't

  • ~190 m of 440 V cable laid underground

  • 6 poles dismantled and returned to BESCOM
  • Footway and road surfaces restored where dug

Comes later

  • Footpaths, kerbs and bollards at the corners (Phase 4)
  • Turning lanes, medians and signals (Phase 4)
  • Brigade IRV and Ambedkar Statue poles (Phase 3)

Questions donors ask
Why are residents paying? Isn't this BESCOM's job?

BESCOM cannot fund the full relocation, but it is contributing towards the cost of the cables. It has also approved 'self-execution': residents fund the rest of the work, and BESCOM supervises it and provides linemen. Waiting for public funding could take years.

Will removing poles end the traffic jam?

Not by itself. The poles are one of several causes. Removing them makes turns smoother and safer, and it clears the road for Phase 4: footpaths, turning lanes and signals.

Is my donation tax-deductible?

Yes. Donations go to Whitefield Rising Trust, which issues 80G receipts. Please share your PAN when you donate so the receipt can be issued.

Who owns and maintains the new cables?

Everything is built to BESCOM specification and handed over to BESCOM, which then owns and maintains it like any other part of its network.

✓ Phase 1 · Completed

The 40 m Chicken Neck on New Temple Road

For years this 40 m stretch near 15 New Temple Road narrowed to a single lane between compound walls, so opposing cars could not pass. The landowner agreed to let the frontage be used as road while keeping the title, and residents paid for and poured the concrete themselves.

◔ Phase 3 · Awaiting approvals

Brigade IRV & Ambedkar Statue junctions

BESCOM equipment at these two junctions takes up the footpath, leaves no room to walk, and creates corners that become garbage spots. The plan is to move it out of the public way.

Brigade IRV junction

A ring main unit, two 250 kVA transformers and a spun pole to move into a 60 × 20 ft space inside the Brigade IRV campus. BESCOM has said it will bear this cost.

◌ Phase 4 · Design stage

A walkable, signalled loop

Once the poles are gone, the space they free can be organised: footpaths people can actually walk on, turning lanes, medians, signals and safe crossings. The first detailed design, for the Shiva Temple junction, is ready.
